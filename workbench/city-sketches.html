From the map

Your model was map data, extruded by an algorithm. So the city is built the same way: the dust lands as a flat map, and every building rises out of its own footprint. The site is made by your method before it says so.

The look
Edges and windows, faces almost empty, the water black. A city at night is its lights.

Detail where it counts. The seven landmarks you liked and Pinnacle@Duxton are drawn with care. Everything else stays simple blocks, which is exactly what a map extrusion gives.

Not a lidar look. No rainbow height colours or sensor rings: those say "tech demo", and they are not how your model was made.

A

Flow

Your name breaks into data, and the page becomes the river it travels down. Each job is a place the data passes through, and each one changes it the way that job did.

Pipes
The stream splits into lanes and runs at 10,000 a second.
Scan
It lands on a roof as raw points and turns to surface where it is scanned.
Schedule
It settles into a calendar, one booking to a slot.
Model
It condenses into a sentence.

Why it could be right. It never stops moving with you, and nothing needs explaining.

The risk. There is no single object to remember the site by.
